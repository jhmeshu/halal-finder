# 02 — Business Model & Market Strategy

## Market entry: focused coverage, global intent
The app should be capable of representing businesses worldwide from day one, but the initial dataset and sales effort should be concentrated. A global product does not require a global launch.

### Suggested test sequence
1. **London:** first commercial and user-research test; manually build a high-quality cluster of listings.
2. **North American expansion candidate:** New York, Toronto/GTA, then another city based on demand and available data.
3. **Australia:** Sydney and Melbourne when there is capacity to maintain evidence and corrections.
4. **Asian travel discovery:** Tokyo/Osaka, Seoul, Bangkok and other traveller-heavy destinations as listing quality allows.
5. Expand to additional European and Asian cities based on searches, user requests, and partner interest.

This is a practical hypothesis, not validated market research. Interview target users and businesses before choosing where to invest effort. Avoid a Bangladesh-first or Islamic-majority-country-first positioning; Bangladesh may be where the founder operates, but it is not the target market defined for this product.

## Competitive landscape and lesson
Existing services already cover parts of the concept: halal place discovery, Muslim travel discovery, barcode/ingredient scanning, and business directories. Examples discussed during planning include Zabihah, HalalTrip, Mustakshif and Halal & Healthy, plus regional business listing services.

The combination alone is not a defensible moat. The likely opportunity is to execute better on:
- Transparent evidence and freshness.
- Clear distinction between business claims and independent checks.
- Integrated place discovery and product checking.
- Useful coverage in selected cities, including Asian travel destinations.
- Fast corrections and accountable moderation.

Public claims about competitor revenue, valuation, user counts or exact feature parity are often inconsistent or not independently confirmed. Do not use uncertain third-party estimates in an investor pitch without validation.

## Revenue options (in priority order)
### 1. Business subscriptions / enhanced profiles
Possible benefits: richer profile, menu/product details, opening hours, photos, offers, response to corrections, analytics. Basic listing should remain free initially to build coverage.

### 2. Clearly labelled sponsored placement
Businesses can pay for visibility, but paid placement must never affect halal evidence status. Establish ad policies for misleading claims, sensitive categories and evidence disputes.

### 3. Optional ad-free premium
Keep core discovery and basic scanner access free at first. Premium could offer saved lists across devices, advanced filters, travel collections, alerts or other features that have proven demand. Do not put safety-critical evidence behind a paywall.

### 4. Relevant advertising
Potential categories include halal food brands, travel services, Muslim-friendly accommodation and related retail. Ads must be labelled and should not influence trust labels.

### 5. Referral / ordering commissions
Consider only after discovery usage and local fulfilment partners are strong enough. Delivery adds operations, disputes, payments and legal complexity, so it should not be part of the MVP.

## Simple revenue model
Monthly gross revenue can be modelled as:

`paid businesses × monthly business fee + premium subscribers × monthly price + ad revenue + referral commissions`

This is a formula, not a forecast. For each market, validate willingness to pay before setting prices. Track gross revenue separately from infrastructure, moderation, support, payment processing, tax and marketing costs.

## Go-to-market plan
- Recruit 10–20 target users for moderated usability sessions.
- Ask halal food communities and travel groups for corrections and unmet-city requests; follow group rules and avoid spam.
- Personally contact a small group of businesses with a clear free listing offer and an evidence checklist.
- Partner with certifiers or trusted community organizations only where their scope and independence are clear.
- Publish explainers on what each evidence status means.
- Ask testers to compare Halal Finder with the tools they already use.

## Decision gates
Do not buy ads or build complex monetization until there is evidence that:
- People can find useful results in the target area.
- Listings are accurate enough to retain trust.
- Users understand the evidence labels.
- A meaningful subset returns to the product.
- Businesses express real interest in paid visibility or tools.
